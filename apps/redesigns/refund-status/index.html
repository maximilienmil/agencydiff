1. Check your refund status

Check your refund status with the Where's My Refund tracker. Have your tax return with you.

You need 3 things from your tax return:

  • Social Security number or ITIN — your Social Security number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number.
  • Filing status and tax year — for example, single or married filing jointly, and the tax year you filed for.
  • Exact refund amount — the exact refund amount shown on your tax return.

When to check: Refund status is available 24 hours after you e-file a current-year return.

Tip: If you filed a paper return, wait until the paper-return timing below before checking. See also: how long refunds take.

Next: timing

2. How long refunds take

An e-filed return typically arrives 3 weeks after the date you e-filed. A mailed return takes 6 or more weeks from the date the IRS received it.
  • Paper-return refund status is available 4 weeks after you file.
  • A refund can be delayed when the return needs corrections or further review.
  • Refunds claiming the Earned Income Credit are held until mid-February.

If the normal time has passed and you still have no refund, see what to do next.

Next: lost or delayed help

3. If your refund is lost, stolen, or delayed

If your refund check was lost or stolen, you can request a refund trace to track it.

Before starting a trace, confirm the full processing time has passed (within 4 weeks of issuance).

  • Automated refund hotline: 800-829-1954 — have your tax return on hand when you call.
  • Joint filers: contact 800-829-1040 or complete and send Form 3911.
  • Check on your phone: You can also check your refund with the IRS mobile app.

For Form 3911, follow the instructions on the form to fill it out and where to send it. Start with the tracker in section 1 if you have not checked status yet.
