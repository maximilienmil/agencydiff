# AgencyDiff: positioning against the research frontier

*Ticket 17 research note. Written 2026-11-01. Sources checked to November 2026, weighting the last 12 months. Every load-bearing claim is cited. Where a claim rests on an abstract only, or on a secondary source, that is said out loud.*

*Read first and used throughout: the project [README](../README.md), the project [GLOSSARY](../GLOSSARY.md), the earlier [prior-art note](<AgencyDiff build risk brief/prior_art.md>), the [build risk brief](../reports/AgencyDiff%20build%20risk%20brief.md), [eval/RESULTS.md](../eval/RESULTS.md), [docs/susbench.md](../docs/susbench.md), and [docs/audit.md](../docs/audit.md).*

> **The loud warning, up front.** Three things we might have wanted to claim are already done, by named people, in print. Do not claim them.
>
> 1. **Agents running on two interface variants and comparing behaviour is done.** AgentA/B already does it, on live pages, at 1,000-agent scale ([AgentA/B, arXiv:2504.09723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09723)).
> 2. **An LLM agent auditing real sites for dark patterns, end to end, is done.** Sun, Vekaria and Nithyanand deployed exactly that across 456 data-broker CCPA request flows in March 2026 ([arXiv:2603.03881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03881)). UMBRA also already traces multi-step consent flows and monitors cookie state, at 14,000-site scale ([arXiv:2603.21515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21515)).
> 3. **A strong model judging screenshots for manipulative design is done — including by our own baseline.** Our judge caught 18 of 20 seeded regressions, with no false alarms, for about two cents ([eval/RESULTS.md](../eval/RESULTS.md)). Detection accuracy is not our edge.
>
> What is left is narrower, and it is real. See the one-sentence claim in section (b).

## (a) Closest prior work

Honest reading: AgencyDiff is a combination, not an invention of any single part. The table says what each neighbour does, and what — if anything — AgencyDiff adds over it.

| Work | What it does | What AgencyDiff adds |
|---|---|---|
| [SusBench (Guo, Hiniker et al., UW, IUI '26), arXiv:2510.11035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11035) | Injects 123 dark patterns into 55 live shopping sites; 313 tasks; measures whether computer-use agents (and 29 humans) fall for them. Humans and agents are both most susceptible to Preselection, Trick Wording and Hidden Information. | Holds a **Protective Intent** fixed and varies the **product version**, not an injected pattern. Reports a deterministic cost/visibility/default diff with an oracle on final state, as a CI gate. SusBench tests the agent; AgencyDiff tests the product. |
| [DECEPTICON (Cuvin, Zhu, Yang, Stanford), arXiv:2512.22894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22894) | 700 tasks (600 generated, 100 real) isolating single dark patterns. Patterns steer agents to the malicious outcome in over 70% of tasks, vs a 31% human average; bigger and more reasoning-heavy models are *more* susceptible; prompting and guardrails do not reliably help. | Uses that susceptibility as a reason **not** to trust the agent's path: success is decided by an Oracle on final app state, never by the agent's claim ([README](../README.md); `packages/core/src/types.ts`). Also: version diff, not agent robustness score. |
| [TrickyArena / LiteAgent (Ersoy et al.), arXiv:2510.18113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18113) | Controlled web apps where dark patterns can be switched on and off. Agents fall for a single pattern about 41% of the time on average; vision input lowers success and raises susceptibility. | Borrows the pattern on/off idea as a *validation* method (our seeded Circle app and the SusBench adapter do this), but the deliverable is a baseline-vs-candidate product diff with replayable Traces, not a susceptibility rate. |
| [Dark Patterns Meet GUI Agents (Tang et al.), arXiv:2509.10723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10723) | Two-phase study of agents, humans and human–AI teams across 16 pattern types. ## (b) The novelty claim

### Say this (one sentence)

> **AgencyDiff is a regression test for a product promise: it holds one paired user Intent fixed, runs it on the baseline and the candidate, and reports — deterministically, from oracle-verified final state and recorded Traces — whether saying no got more expensive, less visible, differently defaulted, or impossible.**

Every word of that is load-bearing, and each clause excludes a neighbour: *regression test* (not audit, not benchmark), *paired* (accept vs Protective Intent, so Asymmetry exists), *product promise* (the Agency Contract is the team's own commitment, not a researcher's taxonomy), *deterministically, from oracle-verified final state* (not a model's opinion — see the judge numbers in section (e)), *recorded Traces* (replayable evidence, per Rossi & Parkin's transparency requirement, [arXiv:2602.16302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16302)).

### We must NOT claim

- **Not** "first to use agents to detect dark patterns." Refuted by SusBench ([arXiv:2510.11035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11035)), DECEPTICON ([arXiv:2512.22894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22894)), Sun et al.'s 456-site audit ([arXiv:2603.03881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03881)) and UMBRA ([arXiv:2603.21515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21515)).
- **Not** "first to compare UI versions with agents." Refuted by AgentA/B ([arXiv:2504.09723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09723)).
- **Not** "first to measure accept/reject asymmetry or first-layer visibility." Regulators defined these tests years ago (regulation section below), and Nouwens et al. measured the effect size in 2020 ([arXiv:2001.02479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02479)).
- **Not** "first dark-pattern check in a PR." DarkLens markets exactly that positioning (see the commercial note above).
- **Not** "agents show what users experience." Agents fall for patterns 41–70% of the time ([arXiv:2510.18113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18113); [arXiv:2512.22894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22894)) and, per Tang et al., prioritise finishing the task over protecting the user ([arXiv:2509.10723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10723)). Our own docs already say it correctly: "The agents are standardized probes, not simulated humans. AgencyDiff measures the interface, not predicted human behaviour" ([README](../README.md)).
- **Not** "finds dark patterns" as the headline. Our own SusBench adapter catches 7 of 10 injected patterns and, by design, misses all three wording/persuasion patterns ([docs/susbench.md](../docs/susbench.md)). A detection claim invites a detection contest we lose to a screenshot judge.
- Avoid the word **"first"** entirely until a dedicated GitHub/open-source sweep for "consent regression", "intent diff" and similar terms has been run. The earlier brief flagged the same gap ([prior-art note](<AgencyDiff build risk brief/prior_art.md>)); it is still open.

## (c) Pitches, ranked

Ranked for a Meta hackathon room first, a shipping product team second. All three are honest under section (b).

### 1. "Regression tests for human choice." ⭐ lead with this

Tagline: *"Your CI checks the code still works. AgencyDiff checks the user can still say no."*

- Why it wins with Meta judges: it is a category claim, not a feature claim, and categories are what "creativity and technical ambition" judging rewards (the official criteria, per the [build risk brief](../reports/AgencyDiff%20build%20risk%20brief.md)). It also maps directly onto a thing Meta already ships and defends: Teen Accounts are, in effect, a promise that protective choices are on by default and reachable — private by default for under-16s, with parental approval needed to loosen settings ([Meta, "Introducing Instagram Teen Accounts", 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/09/instagram-teen-accounts/)). "Did this week's change keep that promise?" is a question that room already cares about.
- Why it wins with product teams: every engineer already believes in regression tests. No new mental model, no ethics lecture. It slots next to Percy/Chromatic in the pipeline they already run.
- Risk: "human choice" must not drift into "we simulate humans." Keep the probe-not-persona line attached.

### 2. "The diff for agency regressions."

Tagline: *"Pixels moved in every redesign. Find out if 'no' moved too."*

- Why: it speaks the visual-regression language teams already buy, then names the gap WUICC-bench itself identifies — pixel comparison is "semantically blind" ([arXiv:2607.01728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01728)). Strongest with engineering and design-systems audiences.
- Why second: it sounds like an add-on to Percy rather than a new discipline, and "agency" needs one sentence of explanation before it lands.

### 3. "Ship the redesign. Keep the promise."

Tagline: *"Write the promise once — 'users can turn off precise location' — and get a failing check the day a redesign buries it."*

- Why: the most concrete and demo-friendly; it is literally what the seeded Circle benchmark shows ([eval/RESULTS.md](../eval/RESULTS.md)). Best cold-open for a 3-minute video: show the promise, ship the buried setting, watch the check fail.
- Why third: it sells one use case (settings/consent) and undersells the general Intent/Contract machinery that makes the project technically ambitious.

**Audience split, in one line each.** Research judges: lead with the probe-not-persona measurement stance and the oracle — it answers the validity attack before it is made. Product/eng judges: lead with the exit code and the Trace link in the PR. Exec/policy judges: lead with the regulation-shaped metrics in the next section.

## (d) Why now

Three forces, each with a citation a sceptic can check.

**1. Computer-use agents are now good enough to be measuring instruments — and bad enough that the instrument must be checked against state, not trusted.** Frontier agents have moved from novelty to 60–70% territory on standard web/desktop benchmarks (Surfer 2 reports 69.6% on WebArena and 60.1% on OSWorld without task-specific fine-tuning, [arXiv:2510.19949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19949)). That is good enough to walk a settings flow repeatably. But the same literature shows agents are steered by dark patterns in over 70% of tasks ([arXiv:2512.22894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22894)). Both halves matter: capable enough to probe, untrustworthy enough that the Oracle — final app state, instrumentation the agent never sees — has to deliver the verdict. Two years ago the first half was false. Now the design is possible *and* forced.

**2. Interfaces are increasingly written by agents, so regressions arrive faster than humans review.** AI-generated interfaces can reproduce and optimise deceptive patterns present in their training data, in subtler and more personalised forms ([arXiv:2602.18445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18445); read at abstract level — treat as directional, not as measured prevalence). The stronger, safer version of this claim is about volume and review bandwidth, not malice: when UI is generated and A/B-tested at machine speed, a human design review per change stops being the control. A deterministic, replayable check that runs per change is the control that scales. Pair this with Rossi & Parkin: regulators already say automated tools are only useful if they are transparent and map to legal duties ([arXiv:2602.16302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16302)).

**3. The law now names the thing being measured, and the next law names it harder.** See the regulation box below. The short version: the EU has prohibited manipulative interface design on platforms since the DSA applied in February 2024 ([DSA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj)), the AI Act's manipulation prohibition has applied since 2 February 2025 ([AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)), and the proposed Digital Fairness Act — expected as a Commission proposal in late 2026 — is set to make dark patterns, addictive design and unfair personalisation its core business ([European Commission, Digital Fairness Act](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/priorities-2019-2024/europe-fit-digital-age/digital-fairness-act_en); proposal timing per [CMS, "Inside the EU's planned Digital Fairness Act"](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/inside-the-eu-s-planned-digital-fairness-act-digital-design-subscription-traps-and-influencer-marketing)). A metric that is already law-shaped does not need to persuade anyone it matters.

## (e) How to present our honest numbers

Lead with the number that hurts. It is the most credible thing we have.

**1. "A screenshot judge is already good. That is not the product."** On the 26-seed Circle benchmark, a Muse judge caught 18/20 real regressions with 0/6 false alarms for $0.02 a run; the scripted AgencyDiff harness caught 20/20 with 0/6 ([eval/RESULTS.md](../eval/RESULTS.md)). Say plainly: if you only want a yes/no on one build, use the judge. Then say what the judge cannot give you:

- **Quantified, not verdicted.** The judge says "harder." The Diff says the Protective Intent went from 2 to 6 actions, navigation depth 0 → 2, Asymmetry 1.0 → 3.0 (`packages/core/src/diff.ts`, `metrics.ts`). A team can set a budget against a number ("no more than 2× asymmetry"); it cannot set one against an adjective.
- **Replayable, not re-rollable.** Every Finding links to Trace ids and steps (`packages/core/src/types.ts`: `Finding.evidence`). Re-running a judge gives you a new opinion (our own k=3 judge consistency was 89% over 18 verdicts, [eval/RESULTS.md](../eval/RESULTS.md)); re-reading a Trace gives you the same evidence.
- **Instrument-measured where the agent is weakest.** Initial visibility and default are read from instrumentation/DOM state, not inferred from whether the agent happened to succeed. The clearest case in our own results: on the SusBench `popup-ad` pair, the success drop partly reflects the probe missing a small Close link, but the visibility Finding rests on the overlay covering the control — instrument-measured, independent of agent skill ([docs/susbench.md](../docs/susbench.md)). And **invisible defaults** — a pre-checked box, a flipped `defaultKey` — are state, not behaviour: no amount of watching an agent act reveals them unless you read the initial state, which the Diff does and a screenshot judge, on our own benchmark, missed (`LOC_DEFAULT_ON` was the judge's clearest miss, [eval/results/judge.json](../eval/results/judge.json)).

**2. Present the scripted 20/20 correctly, or a good judge will do it for you.** The scripted row replays the baseline reference path on both Variants; our docs correctly call it "a sanity check on the harness itself" ([eval/RESULTS.md](../eval/RESULTS.md)). Never present it as agent capability. The honest sentence is: "The harness is verified end-to-end with zero model variance; the model-driven probes and the judge are then scored against the same ground truth."

**3. Present the SusBench result as a scope statement, not a score.** Caught 7 of 10 injected patterns; the three misses (double-negative, confirm-shaming, social proof) change what a page *says*, not what a choice *costs*, and the probe finished each Intent in the same number of steps ([docs/susbench.md](../docs/susbench.md)). Frame: "AgencyDiff measures the cost and reachability of a choice. Persuasion that costs nothing is a real harm and a different instrument." That sentence converts a 7/10 into a definition — but only if we say it before a judge finds the misses.

**4. Own the noise, and name the fix.** One false alarm on 3 no-change controls, caused by agent variance at k=3: two identical pages, one set of repeats took more steps ([docs/susbench.md](../docs/susbench.md)). The `hidden-info` pair even shows inverted action costs (reject: 10 actions baseline vs 4.5 candidate) alongside a valid success-rate detection. The fix is already identified in our docs: require a cost change to exceed the spread across repeats before reporting a Finding. Present k=3 medians as directional, ship the spread gate, and say both things in the same breath. A measurement tool that publishes its own false-alarm mechanism is more trustworthy than one that publishes only a catch rate.

**5. State the black-box limits before they are discovered.** On external pages there is no initial-state key, so default flips cannot fire; `model-judged` success is labelled and can never enter a two-Variant Diff; page-anchored oracles do not transfer to a paraphrasing redesign (the DMV audit's central finding, [docs/audit.md](../docs/audit.md)). Each limit is a design decision with a reason. Listing them is the "whether it works" criterion, answered honestly.

**The one-paragraph version for a slide:**

> A strong screenshot judge already catches most seeded regressions (18/20, $0.02). AgencyDiff is not a better opinion. It is the measurement underneath the opinion: the same Intent, both builds, success decided by the app's own final state, reported as actions, depth, first-screen visibility, default and accept-vs-protective asymmetry — every number replayable from a recorded Trace. On an external dark-pattern benchmark it catches the patterns that make a choice costlier or unreachable (7/10), and by design not the ones that only change the wording. That boundary is the product definition, not an apology.

## (f) Vocabulary: use / avoid

The project glossary is already disciplined ([GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md)). Keep it, and add the external-facing bridges below.

**Use**

| Term | Why |
|---|---|
| **Agency regression** / "regression test for human choice" | Names a *change between versions*, which is the defensible claim. A regression can be accidental; nobody has to be accused of anything. |
| **Agency Contract, Intent, Protective Intent, Intent Pair** | Our terms ([GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md)). "Protective Intent" (decline, restrict, cancel, delete, leave) is the concept neighbours lack; lead with it. |
| **Probe** (not user, not persona) | Encodes the validity stance: a standardised measuring instrument. Directly answers the SusBench/DECEPTICON susceptibility attack. |
| **Oracle, Trace, Finding** | Oracle = final-state check the agent never sees. Trace = replayable evidence. Finding = one measured change, linked to Traces. This trio *is* the credibility story (Rossi & Parkin, [arXiv:2602.16302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16302)). |
| **Asymmetry** (reject cost ÷ accept cost), **initially visible**, **default** | Law-shaped, numeric, checkable. They map to DSA Art. 25 and to Nouwens et al.'s 22–23-point effect ([arXiv:2001.02479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02479)). |
| **"Dark patterns" — once, as a bridge** | It is the term judges, regulators and the literature use. Use it to locate the problem ("the literature calls these dark patterns"), then switch to our measured vocabulary for everything we *report*. |

**Avoid**

| Term | Why |
|---|---|
| "Dark pattern" as a Finding label | Our own glossary already bans it, correctly: a Finding is a measured change, not an accusation of intent. It also picks a fight about motive we cannot prove and do not need. |
| "Synthetic user", "simulated user", "persona", "digital twin of your customers" | That is UXAgent/AgentA/B/PerceptUI's claim ([arXiv:2504.09407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09407); [arXiv:2606.05697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05697)), and it carries the human-proxy validity burden. We measure interfaces. |
| "Agency score", "UX score", "compliance score" | A single blended number hides exactly the structure (which Intent, which metric, which Trace) that makes the output trustworthy. Never blend. |
| "Violation", "illegal", "non-compliant" | We measure cost, visibility and defaults. Whether that breaches DSA Art. 25 is a legal judgement for lawyers; Rossi & Parkin's regulators want tools that *map to* legal duties, not tools that pronounce them ([arXiv:2602.16302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16302)). |
| "First", "only", "nobody has…" | Refuted in section (b) for every plausible completion of that sentence. |
| "The agent found…", "the AI decided…" (for verdicts) | Verdicts are computed deterministically from Traces and Oracles. The agent only walks. Say "the Diff reports", "the Oracle failed". |
| "Users will…" (any behavioural prediction) | Agent paths are not human paths; susceptibility runs 41–70% ([arXiv:2510.18113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18113)). Report interface cost; let humans be studied by humans. |
